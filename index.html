About

I am a research scientist specializing in the integration of geospatial data, geotechnical analysis, and hydrologic modeling. My work bridges atmospheric remote sensing, hydrologic forecasting, and geotechnical risk assessment. Early in my research career, I developed GNSS-based near–real-time rainfall forecasting models using machine learning techniques, establishing a foundation in signal processing and predictive modeling. I later expanded this expertise toward scalable flood prediction and automated geospatial data pipelines for river ice monitoring. My Ph.D. research focused on identifying the key drivers of land subsidence in urban coastal regions through integrated InSAR analysis and subsurface investigations. Following a summer internship with Freeport-McMoRan Inc., I transitioned these methods into mining geotechnics, developing a rapid InSAR-based monitoring data stream prototype, which is used for early risk identification in tailings storage facilities and slope stability in open-pit mining operations.
